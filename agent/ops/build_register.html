SharePoint folders can be built but not shared

Blocked 30 manual shares, once
Why
The M365 connector has create_folder, upload_file, move_item, copy_item, rename_item and delete_item — and no permission or sharing call at all. Checked against the full tool surface, not assumed. So the agent can build the whole structure and cannot grant one person access to it.
Mirror image of Drive
On Google Drive the agent can grant access and never revoke it. On SharePoint it can do neither. Both halves of sharing are manual.
Why it is survivable
This is exactly why folder-per-caller beats folder-per-date. Thirty shares, once, forever — against twenty to forty every single day under the other shape. A one-off cost is the difference between a system that runs and one that quietly stops.
How to share correctly
"Specific people", never "Anyone with the link" — the latter is forwardable and is how the Google call sheets ended up open to the world. Share the individual folder, never the parent Call Sheets folder, or everyone gets everyone's numbers.

Measuring a shift from the call logs — built and tested

Working Calibrated
What it does
business_agent/calllogs.py groups a day's Zoom Phone calls per caller and reports, for each: attempts, answered, talk time, completes, short answers, each break and their total, time actually worked, and how far short of a full shift. 77 tests on the module, 543 across the agent, all of which run with no Zoom connection at all. This is the half of Elaine's audit that was manual.
Completes
Zoom cannot say whether a questionnaire was finished — only that someone picked up and stayed on. So length stands in for it: an answered call of 150 seconds or more counts as a complete. That is Brendon's figure and it is a parameter, not a law: too low and a polite refusal counts, too high and genuinely quick respondents are thrown away. Calibrated 11 September against Elaine's own audit of 10 September — and 150s is the figure she already uses. See the entry below; this is settled.
The trap that drove the design
One of Pernelia's calls ran 6 minutes 27 seconds. Measuring the gap between one call starting and the next would have booked those six minutes as time away from the phone — punishing her for the longest conversation of the day. Breaks are therefore measured from the end of one call to the start of the next, and time inside a call is never idle however long it ran. Overlapping calls are handled with a running latest-end, so a short call finishing inside a longer one cannot invent a gap that never happened.
The idle floor
A gap under 60 seconds is not a break — it is hanging up, reading the next number and dialling. Counting those would make the fastest caller read as the idlest, because a fast caller has more of them. Brendon set the floor at 60s (it was 120s). Breaks are returned individually as well as totalled: three twenty-minute breaks and forty two-minute ones add to the same number and mean completely different things.
Three hours, not a clock window
A shift is three hours of calling, so lateness is not the measure — the length is. The clock starts at a caller's own first call, floored at 1:30pm Manila so one stray call at noon cannot run the three hours out before the work began, and it ends when the last call ends, not when it starts. Shortfall is measured against time worked rather than elapsed time, so three hours cannot be covered by one call, a disappearance and another call.
Run against a real day
Done, 11 September, across 1–11 September: 33,875 calls, 37 Zoom display names, and the Manila boundary correct — Pernelia's 10 September shift lands 13:25–16:26 Manila, inside the right date. Caller names come back on every outbound row. The three checks all pass.
Correction: completes were reading zero
A claim in this register was wrong, and wrong in the quietest possible way. ANSWERED in calllogs.py listed Connected, Answered and Call connected — a guess at Zoom's vocabulary. Against 11 days of live data two of those three never appear at all and the third caps at 7 seconds, so it is a dialler state and not a conversation. Every call with real talk time comes back as Auto Recorded, which the module was treating as unanswered.
The real values
Auto Recorded 15,669 (median 14s, max 2,841s) · Call Cancel 11,457 (all 0s) · Call connected 5,579 (median 3s, max 7s) · Call failed 5. On the old set the module scored 0 completes for every caller on every day, and talk_seconds and short_answers with it. The timing work — breaks, idle time, time worked, shortfall — never read answered and was never affected.
Now fixed
ANSWERED is {"Auto Recorded", "Connected", "Answered"}: Auto Recorded added, Call connected dropped — counting it would have invented 5,579 answered calls nobody ever spoke on — and the other two kept only for a tenant on a different API version. That gives 953 completes at 150s across the 11 days. Each value now has a test naming what it is, so the next person does not have to rediscover it. Left unfixed at first, deliberately, and that was the weaker call: the numbers it protected were all zero.
Still Brendon's to confirm
That Auto Recorded is what Zoom's own reporting calls answered. Everything downstream of it — completes, talk time, short answers — moves if it is not, and it is one line back. The timing figures never read it.
One more found
calls_for_day passed its default empty token straight to Zoom, so a real call sent Bearer  and came back 401 — which reads like a bad credential rather than a missing one, and would have cost someone an hour. It now mints its own token when no test stub is supplied.
On the 150s figure
It sits in a flat part of the curve, not on a cliff: 1,753 answered calls land 30–149s and 953 at 150s or more. That was as far as the data could settle it. Elaine's audit settled the rest — she flags a survey "below 2 min and 30 seconds", which is the same 150s. See the calibration entry below.

And that one cannot be read
Graph refuses to extract it:
notSupported — Microsoft blocks conversion for applications that cannot decrypt the file. Per-file, enforced by Microsoft; retrying will not help unless the file or its label changes.
What that means
Normally an encrypting Purview sensitivity label. Brendon can still open the file in a browser under his own login — this is not a permissions problem, it is Microsoft declining to hand the bytes to an application. An earlier entry read the same notSupported as routine spreadsheet behaviour and concluded it "says nothing about whether Excel can open the file". That was right about Excel and wrong about the consequence: it is the reason the declared half of an audit cannot be read at all, and it is now a blocker rather than a curiosity.
Consequence for the audit
The audit compares what a caller declared against what the phone recorded. Zoom supplies the second half in full. The first half has no source: the sheets are mostly not there, and the ones that are cannot be opened. Until the label question is settled, declared numbers have to come from WhatsApp end-of-day or Curia's own reporting, not from these files.
Folder names carry the nickname
Pernelia Villapaz (Lia), Mary Joy Tongson (Mary T), Mary Joy Villacura (Mary V). A path built from the plain name 404s — five spellings were tried before the folder was found by listing rather than guessing. Never construct these paths; list the parent.
How to list it
read_resource on file:///{driveId}/root, then follow the item IDs. A single-segment path such as file:///{driveId}/Call Sheets is parsed as an item ID, not a path, and dies with invalidRequest — which reads like a broken drive and is not. Search is no help either: the tenant index holds one file from this whole site.

Messaging a caller about their own numbers — not on this metric yet

Needs a decision
The intent
Run the audit about an hour after the shift ends — finish 5pm, audit 6pm — and message a caller automatically about time off the phone. The timing is sound. Zoom logs are complete the moment the shift ends and results land the same day, so an hour is comfortable.
The problem is the number
Time off the phone is the one figure that does not reconcile with the manual audit. Completes agree on 16 of 22 callers; off-phone agrees on one in six, and this module reads up to four times higher on the same caller and day — Eunilyn at 81 minutes against Elaine's 18. Every other number in the system has been checked against a hand count and holds. This one has been checked and does not.
Why that is the wrong thing to automate first
"You were off the phone for 43 minutes" is an accusation, not a statistic. Sent automatically, to an employee, with no person reading it first, built on the least reliable figure in the build — and if the existing manual method says 20 minutes, the caller is right and the agent is wrong. One of those messages costs more trust than the whole audit saves.
The order to do it in
Reconcile the off-phone measure first — decide what a break is, since Elaine's figure is a judgement and not a floor. Then draft the messages. Then Brendon sends them, or approves a batch. Draft everything, send nothing has not been relaxed, and this is exactly the case it exists for: untrusted-adjacent output going to ~40 people about their own pay and standing.
What is safe now
The same loop with completes rather than off-phone time — that figure is calibrated, 96.6% against the hand count. And the whole thing drafted for Brendon to read and send, which costs him one action a day rather than twenty.

Slack free plan: 90 days of history

Plan for it
Not the problem
No member cap. The "1,000 users" figure is not the binding limit — a free workspace takes the 100–150 people Pacific Link expects. App installs cap at 10 (Claude is one) and huddles are 1-to-1, neither of which bites here.
The real limit
Message history is readable for 90 days. After that it is gone — to Brendon and to the agent alike.
Harmless for
Availability and rostering. Standing-pattern derivation looks back 56 days, comfortably inside 90.
Harmful for
The results channel. Deciding who to keep on a year of performance will not work if the year has been eaten. The fix is not paying Slack — it is extracting results weekly into Pacific Link's own records, where they are permanent and not rented. That belongs in the weekly routine.
